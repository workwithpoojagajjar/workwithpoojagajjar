Training Is a Business Investment

A perspective by Pooja Gajjar · October 2026

Training is sometimes treated as a task to complete before an employee starts working independently. I see it as a continuing investment in the quality and reliability of the business.

Confidence changes execution

Employees who understand the standards, the reasons behind them, and how to respond when something goes wrong are better equipped to make decisions during service.

Simple tools can be powerful

Clear checklists, demonstrations, feedback, and reinforcement can help people apply what they learn. Training material should make work easier to perform, not create another layer of paperwork.

Measure the right signals

Businesses can evaluate training through practical signals such as error patterns, consistency, employee readiness, and guest feedback. Those signals need careful interpretation, but they help move the conversation beyond attendance at a session.

My experience coaching restaurant teams reinforced a simple lesson: when businesses invest in helping people succeed, operational performance has a stronger foundation.
